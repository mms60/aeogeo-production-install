The search box stopped being a list of links

Someone researching your services today may never see your homepage. They ask an assistant a question, read a single answer, and move on. That answer is assembled from websites the assistant could actually fetch, parse and trust.

That is the whole game now. Not a position on a page of blue links — whether a machine can retrieve your content, work out what it is looking at, and describe your business accurately to the person asking.

What quietly goes wrong when nobody checks

  • Your robots.txt blocks an AI crawler and nobody notices, because human visitors are unaffected.
  • Your most persuasive copy is rendered by JavaScript, so the crawler receives a near-empty page.
  • A noindex tag left over from the staging site is still live on your most profitable service page.
  • You publish no structured data, so the machine has to guess what you sell, where you are, and who you serve.
  • Your sitemap is out of date, so whole sections of the site are effectively invisible.
  • You find out six months later, when a competitor is the one being recommended.

None of this shows up in a browser. It only shows up when you look at your website the way a crawler does — which is exactly what this workspace is built to do.

Run the check on my site

Two questions decide whether AI can use your website

Most tools answer one of them. You need both, because a site that is perfectly readable but says nothing machine-parsable is just as invisible as one that is blocked entirely.

Question one: is AI actually allowed in?

Your website can be open to visitors and closed to machines. A single line in a robots.txt file, a firewall rule, a redirect chain or a login wall can stop an AI crawler long before it reads a word of your content.

The AI crawler audit requests your pages using representative crawler user agents and shows you what came back — page by page, in plain English.

What we check, and why each one matters

  • Crawler permissions. We read your robots.txt the way an AI crawler does. If the rules block one provider while allowing another, you will see it instead of assuming.
  • Access and response codes. Errors, soft 404s and redirect chains are dead ends. If a crawler cannot get a clean 200, it has nothing to quote.
  • Content in the returned HTML. This is the big one. We check whether your headline, service descriptions and key facts are actually present in what the server sends — not just what renders after JavaScript runs.
  • JavaScript dependence. Pages that only exist after the browser executes scripts are fragile for crawlers, and we flag the ones at risk.
  • Indexing directives. An inherited noindex or a stray canonical pointing at the wrong URL can quietly remove a page from consideration entirely.
  • Sitemap discovery. Your sitemap is the map you hand a crawler. If it is missing, broken or stale, pages stay unfound.

You get a prioritised list of what to fix, in the order worth fixing it — not a wall of green ticks.

Question two: does your website say what your business is?

Even when a crawler can read every word, it still has to interpret them. Is this a local plumber or a national franchise? Is that a product or a service? Which location, which price range, which person is the author?

Structured data answers those questions explicitly. Without it, an AI system is inferring your business from prose — and inference is where accuracy quietly falls apart.

Audit what you already publish

  • Detect the JSON-LD already on your page, including blocks added by plugins you may have forgotten about.
  • See which schema types are present, and which obvious ones are missing.
  • Find the pages that carry no structured data at all — usually the ones closest to the money.
  • Spot conflicting or incomplete markup that sends mixed signals about your business.

Generate markup built on your facts

Describe the page in your own words — who you are, what you offer, where you are, what the page is about — and the studio drafts the matching JSON-LD. It is generated from the details you provide, not poured from a generic template that says “Your Company Name Here”.

A page that states plainly what it is gets used. A page that leaves machines guessing gets skipped.

Straight answers before you subscribe

If your question is not here, ask us — we would rather answer it than have you buy the wrong thing.

Is this just your free robots.txt tool with a price tag?

No. The free robots.txt tool helps you write or review that one file. This workspace does something different: it requests your pages as AI crawlers would, reports what came back, audits the structured data already published, and generates new JSON-LD. Think of robots.txt as the door — this checks whether the room behind it is readable.

Will this get me mentioned by ChatGPT?

Nobody can promise that, and any tool that does is selling you something it cannot deliver. What this does is remove the technical reasons an AI system cannot use your site: blocked crawlers, unreadable pages, and missing structured context. Without that foundation, being mentioned is luck. With it, you have done the part you control.

Do the crawler tests prove a real crawler visited my site?

No, and we will not pretend otherwise. The audit sends requests using representative crawler user agents. Proving that a specific provider’s actual IP addresses reached your server requires inspecting your server or CDN logs. The audit tells you what your site returns to that class of request, which is where almost every fixable problem lives.

Can I use the generated schema straight away?

Review it first. The markup is built from the details you type in, so its accuracy depends on the accuracy you gave it. Check names, URLs, addresses, prices and dates, validate it, then publish. Treat the draft as a strong starting point written by someone who knows the format — not as a substitute for reading it.

Will this change or break my live website?

No. The audit is read-only: it fetches public pages and inspects what comes back. Nothing is written to your site, no plugin is installed, and no files are modified. You decide what to implement, and when.

My site runs on WordPress, Shopify, Wix or Squarespace. Does it still work?

Yes — anything publicly reachable over HTTPS. In fact plugin-generated schema is one of the most common things we find: either missing entirely, duplicated three times, or contradicting itself. The audit will show you which.

Do I need a developer?

Not to run the audit, and not to understand the findings — they are written in plain English with a recommendation attached. Adding JSON-LD usually means pasting a snippet into a header, theme file or a schema plugin, which many site owners can do. For anything structural, you will at least hand your developer a precise ticket instead of a vague worry.

I already pay for an SEO tool. Why add this?

Most SEO platforms are built around Google’s crawler and classic ranking data. This focuses on a narrower, newer question: what do AI crawlers get, and does your structured data actually describe your business? Use both. One tracks visibility; this one checks whether the technical door is open in the first place.

How long does an audit take, and what do you store?

A scan of ten to twenty pages usually completes in a few minutes; you can keep working while it runs. Results, findings and generated drafts are stored in your own account, visible only to you. Add the results to your own documentation if you want a permanent copy.

What is it not for?

It is not a site behind a login, an intranet, or a page that only exists in a staging environment. It will not design your content strategy, write your copy, or guarantee rankings, rich results or AI citations. And it will not replace a human review of the markup before it goes live — that part stays yours.
