How Do You Get Your Website on Bing Search?

By AEOGEO Search

Quick Answer

Getting a website onto Bing Search takes three steps: create a Bing Webmaster Tools account, verify ownership of your site, and submit a sitemap so Bing can find your pages. Like Google, Bing decides independently whether to crawl and index each page. Webmaster Tools gets you discovered and lets you monitor what happens next, but it doesn't force indexing or rankings.

Bing matters more than many businesses assume. Bing feeds results to Yahoo Search, DuckDuckGo, and Ecosia, and it powers Copilot and part of the retrieval behind ChatGPT search. A site invisible to Bing is invisible to every surface built on it.

Before You Start: Check Whether Bots Can Crawl Your Site

Before you set up Bing Webmaster Tools, confirm that Bingbot can fetch your pages. A misconfigured robots.txt file that tells crawlers to stay away can hide the whole site, and no verification or sitemap step will fix that. Use our free Robots Text Generator to test access and create a corrected file. Our robots.txt guide for AI bots explains the rules behind the check, including blocks that happen outside robots.txt.

What's Bing Webmaster Tools?

Bing Webmaster Tools (BWT) is a free tool from Microsoft that shows you how your website appears in Bing Search. It's the Bing equivalent of Google Search Console and is aimed at site owners, not advertisers. Microsoft doesn't charge for any part of it.

BWT tells you three things that matter for visibility:

  • Whether Bing knows your pages exist. The URL Inspection and Index Explorer reports show which pages Bing has crawled and indexed, and how your site structure is organized.
  • What people search before clicking. The Search Performance report shows the queries where your site appeared, along with impressions, clicks, and average position.
  • Whether anything is broken. BWT reports crawl errors, SEO recommendations, malware and security issues, and technical problems such as blocked resources.

Bing Webmaster Tools lives at https://www.bing.com/webmasters. Sign in with a Microsoft, Google, or Facebook account.

Step 1: Add Your Site

When you add a website, BWT offers three ways to start:

Import from Google Search Console

If your site is already verified in GSC, this option copies your site over and pulls your sitemap in one step. It's the fastest route for most businesses. Open Google Search Console.

Add Site Manually

Enter your website address and verify ownership directly. This works even if you've never touched Google Search Console.

Bulk Add Multiple Sites

Useful for agencies managing several client websites at once.

If you already set up Google Search Console, importing saves the most time. If you skipped our Google guide and are starting here, use manual add.

Step 2: Verify Ownership

Verification proves to Microsoft that you control the site. The most common methods are below.

XML File

Download the verification file BWT provides and upload it to your site's root folder.

Meta Tag

Paste a meta tag into the <head> section of your homepage.

CNAME Record

Add a DNS record at your domain registrar. Useful when you can't modify page code.

Via Your Hosting Platform

Many hosts and website builders offer guided Bing verification alongside their Google verification options.

Do Not Remove the Verification Token

As with Google, don't remove the verification token after verifying. BWT re-checks ownership periodically, and removing it can cut off your access and your data history.

Step 3: Submit Your Sitemap

If your sitemap is not ready, the XML sitemap and crawl-depth guide explains what belongs in it and how to validate it. The file should return XML at a public URL before you submit it.

Submit It in BWT

Submit the sitemap in BWT under Sitemaps: type the URL of your sitemap file and click submit. From then on, BWT reports the discovery date, last crawl date, and submission status for each sitemap.

Submitting a sitemap is a request, not a command. Pages get indexed when Bing's systems decide they're worth crawling. Fresh, useful, well-linked pages get picked up faster than duplicate or thin ones.

Step 4: Speed Up Indexing with IndexNow

Bing supports IndexNow, a protocol that tells search engines instantly when a page is added or changed, instead of waiting for a scheduled crawl. This is the biggest difference from the Google workflow, because Bing indexes far more quickly when IndexNow is in place.

Three ways to use it:

Automatic

Most major CMS platforms and many hosting providers have IndexNow plugins or built-in support. Enable it once and every publish pings Bing.

Manual

Submit individual URLs through the URL Submission feature in BWT.

API

Your developer can ping IndexNow directly when content changes. It requires generating a key hosted on your site.

For a typical small business, enabling an IndexNow plugin in your CMS is the practical choice. It also benefits any other search engine that supports the protocol.

Step 5: Monitor and Act on What You See

Once verified, give BWT a few days to accumulate data, then build a light routine around it:

Weekly

Check the Crawl Control and crawl error reports for pages Bing failed to fetch, and fix what the reports highlight (blocked resources, redirects, slow response times).

Monthly

Review Search Performance for queries gaining or losing clicks, and check the SEO Analysis report for on-page recommendations on pages that matter.

Whenever You Publish or Change Something Important

Use URL Inspection to confirm the page is reachable and trigger a submission.

What Bing Webmaster Tools Doesn't Do

Honest limits, so expectations stay grounded:

  • It doesn't guarantee indexing. Bing indexes pages at its discretion; submission only improves discovery.
  • It doesn't affect rankings directly. There's no paid or expedited tier; rankings depend on relevance, competition, and site quality.
  • It doesn't show AI chatbot visibility. BWT covers traditional Bing Search results. Citations inside Copilot or ChatGPT answers and referral traffic from AI chatbots need to be measured separately.
  • Traffic volume differs. Bing's share of search traffic is smaller than Google's in most markets, so expect smaller numbers. The value is in the surfaces Bing powers, not raw volume alone.

Key Takeaways

  1. Check crawl access before anything else. Our free Robots Text Generator shows your crawl strength and produces a corrected robots.txt in minutes.
  2. If you already use Google Search Console, import from GSC and finish in minutes.
  3. IndexNow is Bing's fast lane. Enable it once in your CMS and every new page is submitted automatically.
  4. Bing isn't just Bing. Its index feeds Yahoo, DuckDuckGo, Ecosia, Copilot, and part of ChatGPT search, so one setup covers several discovery channels.
  5. Set up both Google and Bing. They're independent indexes with separate crawlers, and appearing in both doubles your coverage.
