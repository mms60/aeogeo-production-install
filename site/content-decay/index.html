Content Decay Doesn't Happen All at Once. That's Why It's Easy to Miss.

A page doesn't have to disappear from search results to become less valuable.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's content decay?

Content decay is the gradual loss of relevance, usefulness, or search performance of previously published website content. It can happen as information becomes outdated, customer expectations change, or competing content becomes more comprehensive.

Why should I analyze my existing website content?

Your existing content represents an investment. Reviewing it can help identify pages that deserve updating, opportunities to provide more useful information, and resources that could be repurposed instead of creating everything from scratch.

Does old content always need to be replaced?

No. Older content may still have considerable value. Updating information, expanding explanations, improving organization, and addressing new customer questions may be more appropriate than replacing an entire page.

Can outdated content affect my search visibility?

It can. Content that becomes less relevant, useful, or competitive may experience declining search performance. However, not every performance decline is caused by content decay, and other technical or competitive factors should also be considered.

Why does content decay matter for AI search?

AI-powered search tools rely on information they can discover and interpret. Accurate, relevant, comprehensive content helps communicate your business's expertise and answer potential customers' questions. Maintaining your content is an important part of your broader search strategy, although no update guarantees AI citations.

Should I create new content or update what I already have?

It depends on your website's needs. If you already have useful information that needs improvement, refreshing it may be the better investment. If your website is missing important topics or customer questions entirely, creating new content may be necessary.

How often should I review my content?

That depends on your industry, competition, and how quickly your information changes. Regular reviews help you identify opportunities before outdated information becomes a bigger problem.

Who should use the Content Decay Tool?

Business owners, marketing professionals, content managers, SEO specialists, and agencies that want to understand where existing website content may need attention and how to get more value from their content investments.
