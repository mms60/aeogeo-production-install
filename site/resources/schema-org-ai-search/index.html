Schema.org for AI Search: The JSON-LD Types That Matter

By AEOGEO Search

Quick answer

Structured data does not guarantee a citation, but it removes guesswork. JSON-LD tells AI systems what your business is, what a page covers, who wrote it, and which URL is canonical, using facts they can check against the visible page.

AI systems do not read Schema.org the way Google reads rich-result markup. There is no switch that turns JSON-LD into a recommendation. What markup does is reduce ambiguity: it states your entity name, its relationships, and the facts behind a page in a format a machine can parse without inference.

What structured data actually solves

When an assistant summarizes a business, it has to decide which organization a page belongs to, which page is the canonical version, and whether a claim comes from the page or from somewhere else. Consistent JSON-LD answers those questions before the model starts guessing. It also connects your pages into one entity through @id values and sameAs links.

The types that carry the most weight

You do not need every type in the vocabulary. These cover most sites and most pages:

  • Organization: name, url, logo, sameAs, address, areaServed and knowsAbout.
  • LocalBusiness: for a physical location, with address, telephone, openingHours and geo.
  • WebSite: site name and URL, which separates the brand from an individual page.
  • BreadcrumbList: the page hierarchy and the name of each level.
  • Article or BlogPosting: headline, author, datePublished, dateModified and image.
  • Person: the author, with sameAs links to verifiable profiles.
  • Service or Product: what you sell, who it is for, and the offer.
  • FAQPage: question and answer pairs, with the caveat below.

The Organization node is the anchor

Put one Organization node on the homepage and reference it by @id from every other page. Give it the same name, logo, phone, address and social profiles that appear in your footer and Google Business Profile. Mismatches between markup and visible content are worse than no markup, because they teach the system that your data is unreliable.

sameAs is not a ranking list. It is an identity list. Link only profiles that clearly belong to the same business.

Article markup: dates and authorship

For guides and articles, dateModified is the field AI systems use to judge freshness. Update it when the content actually changes, not on every deploy. Author markup should point to a real person or organization with a verifiable profile, not a generic byline.

FAQPage markup: still useful, with limits

Google restricted FAQ rich results in 2023 to a small set of authoritative government and health sites. That does not make FAQPage markup useless. The pairs still state questions and answers explicitly, which helps retrieval systems match a page to a query. Do not expect a rich result, and do not mark up questions that are not visible on the page.

Mistakes that cancel the benefit

  • Markup injected only by JavaScript after the page loads, where most AI crawlers never see it.
  • Several Organization nodes that disagree about the name, phone number or address.
  • Review markup for reviews that are not displayed on the page.
  • Marking up URLs that are noindex, redirected or duplicated.
  • Copying a template and leaving another company's @id or logo in place.

How to validate

Run the URL through the Schema Markup Validator for syntax and the Rich Results Test for Google eligibility. Then view the raw HTML, not the rendered DOM, to confirm the JSON-LD is in the initial response. If your markup only appears after hydration, most crawlers will never see it.

Where the markup belongs

Put sitewide entities on the homepage: one Organization node and one WebSite node, linked by @id. Page-level types belong on the pages they describe: Article on a guide, Service on a service page, BreadcrumbList wherever there is a hierarchy. You can place them in one script tag with an @graph array or in several tags; both parse the same way.

Delivery matters more than layout. If a tag manager injects JSON-LD after a consent click, or a client-side router adds it after hydration, crawlers that do not execute JavaScript never see it. Render it with the page.

What markup cannot fix

Structured data does not create facts. If the phone number changes, the markup has to change with it. It will not compensate for thin content, a missing author, or a page that times out. It also cannot force a citation. It only makes the facts you already publish easier to reconcile, which is the part you control.

A short monthly check

Compare the Organization node with the footer, the Google Business Profile and LinkedIn. Confirm that every Article URL returns 200, that dateModified matches the visible update date, and that no page carries a second Organization node with different data. Five minutes of consistency work prevents most entity confusion.

Start with Organization, WebSite, BreadcrumbList and Article. Those four cover most sites and most pages. Add Service, LocalBusiness or Product where they genuinely describe what you offer. Keep every field true.

Key takeaways

  • Put JSON-LD in the initial HTML, not injected later by client-side JavaScript.
  • Use one Organization node, referenced by @id, with facts that match the visible page.
  • dateModified should reflect real edits, not every deploy.
