Meta Tags and Open Graph for AI Answers

By AEOGEO Search

Quick answer

Titles and descriptions are the labels AI systems reuse when they name and link your page. Canonicals decide which URL gets credit. Open Graph tags shape the preview when a link is shared in a chat or an app.

A model summarizing a page has to choose a name for it. The title tag, the H1 and the meta description are the shortest, most reliable statements of what the page is about. When they disagree, the system picks one and your page becomes harder to describe accurately.

The title tag

  • One clear topic, front-loaded.
  • Brand at the end, separated by a pipe or hyphen.
  • Under about 60 characters so it is not truncated.
  • No keyword lists and no repeated city names.

The description is a summary, not a ranking lever

Meta descriptions do not directly change rankings, but they are frequently reused in snippets and AI summaries. Write a plain sentence that states what the page covers and who it is for. Avoid claims the page does not support; a description that overpromises gets rewritten or ignored.

Canonical tags

One URL per topic. The canonical should be absolute, self-referencing on the canonical page, and consistent with internal links and the sitemap. If several URLs serve the same content, pick one and point the rest at it. Conflicting canonicals split signals and confuse retrieval.

Robots meta tags

Use index,follow on pages you want found. Check for noindex left over from staging, and remember that noindex also removes a page from AI retrieval. If you need to hide a page from search but keep it for users, use authentication instead of noindex.

Open Graph and Twitter cards

  • og:title and og:description, matching the page.
  • og:image at 1200 by 630 pixels with a safe margin.
  • og:url with the canonical URL.
  • og:type, usually article or website.
  • twitter:card set to summary_large_image.

Consistency is the point

The title, description, H1 and first paragraph should describe the same page in the same terms. When an assistant quotes your description, it should still be true after the page changes. Review metadata whenever content changes, not only at launch.

Titles that survive rewriting

Search engines and AI systems rewrite titles when they are vague, stuffed or disconnected from the query. A specific title with the topic first and the brand last is less likely to be replaced. Avoid all-caps, repeated separators and the same title on every page.

Descriptions that get quoted

A good description reads like the first sentence of a summary: what the page covers, who it is for, and what the reader will get. It should make sense without the title. If it repeats the title word for word, it adds nothing.

H1 and first paragraph

The H1 and the opening paragraph are what a retrieval system reads first. Keep the H1 close to the title, and answer the page's main question in the first two sentences. A page that spends three paragraphs on background before stating its topic is harder to summarize.

A pre-publish checklist

  • Title under 60 characters and unique to the page.
  • Description under 155 characters and specific.
  • One canonical, self-referencing.
  • index,follow unless the page is intentionally excluded.
  • og:title, og:description, og:image and og:url set.
  • H1 and first paragraph aligned with the title.

Clear labels do not earn citations on their own. They make every citation that does happen more accurate.

Key takeaways

  • Title, H1 and description should describe the same page.
  • One canonical URL per topic, referenced consistently.
  • Open Graph tags control how links look when shared.
